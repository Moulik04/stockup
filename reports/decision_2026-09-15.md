Reorder policy simulated cost, same folds/models as the backtest report. lead_time_days=7, service_level_target=95%, holding_cost_rate=2.0%, stockout_penalty_per_unit=$5.00.

Two service measures are reported throughout: **fill rate** (units shipped ÷ units demanded) and **cycle service level** (share of replenishment cycles with no stockout between placing an order and receiving it). The policy's `service_level_target` is a cycle service level, so CSL is the measure it is actually aiming at; fill rate is what the business feels. They are different quantities and a 95% CSL does not imply a 95% fill rate.

## Second policy: target the fill rate directly

Same forecasts, same residual calibration, same simulation — the only change is that safety stock is sized from the loss function to hit a 95% *fill rate* (`G(k) = (1 − FR) · Q / σ_LT`, with Q the expected demand per cycle) instead of a 95% cycle service level. Because this project sets S = s, Q is small, which is what forces the larger safety factor.

Averaged across models, targeting fill rate directly moves realised fill rate 81.5% → 72.7% and mean total cost $14,848 → $17,171 per fold (+2,323, +15.6%).

Both policies are kept: the comparison is the point. The CSL policy is the cheaper, simpler, more explainable one and stays the default; the fill-rate policy is what to reach for when the business states its service target in units shipped.
